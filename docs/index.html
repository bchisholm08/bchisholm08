Auditory Perception • Psychophysics • Neural Prostheses

How do listeners pull meaning out of competing sound?

I am a researcher at the University of Minnesota, working in the Sensory Optimization & Neural Implant Coding Lab and the Center for Applied & Translational Sensory Science. I study how listeners extract signals from noisy environments, and how that ability changes with hearing loss, age, or an auditory prosthesis.

My work spans psychophysics in cochlear-implant and auditory-nerve-implant recipients, EEG and pupillometry in human listeners, and masking-release experiments in an animal model.

What I do

My work combines experimental design with hands-on analysis. I run psychophysical and behavioral experiments, validate stimulus hardware, and build analysis pipelines in R and MATLAB using mixed-effects and generalized additive models. I care about rigorous measurement and about making results legible to people outside the lab.
