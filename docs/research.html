Research

I am interested in how listeners extract meaningful signals from competing sound, and how that ability changes with hearing loss, age, or an auditory prosthesis. The projects below approach that question at different levels — an animal model, human listeners, and implant recipients.

I pair behavioral psychophysics with physiological measures, mainly EEG and pupillometry, and analyze the resulting data with mixed-effects and generalized additive models in R and MATLAB.

Projects

Psychophysics for an auditory nerve implant

Sensory Optimization & Neural Implant Coding Lab · PI: Hubert Lim · 2026–present

I validate a six-module psychophysical test suite for an auditory nerve implant, verifying stimulus output and response hardware before protocols go to the collaborating clinical team in Hannover, Germany. I also run recruitment and data collection for a pitch-scaling study in normal-hearing and cochlear-implant listeners, which establishes comparison data for the same task in implant recipients.

Listening effort and fatigue in speech-in-noise

Auditory Perception & Cognition Lab · PI: Andrew Oxenham · 2024–2026

I worked on speech-in-speech perception using EEG and pupillometry, including sessions with listeners experiencing mild cognitive impairment. A first-author manuscript from this work asks whether pupillometric fatigue interacts with experimental manipulation in a listening effort task.

Masking release in Cope’s gray treefrog

Animal Communication Lab · PI: Mark Bee · 2024

I contributed to NSF-funded work on spatial release from masking and comodulation masking release, running two-alternative phonotaxis tests across noise conditions that varied in location and envelope modulation. These are the same perceptual phenomena studied in human speech-in-noise research, in a comparative model of signal detection in noisy environments.

Community hearing and vision screening

Center for Applied & Translational Sensory Science · 2026–present

I administer free hearing and vision screenings aboard the See, Hear, MN mobile sensory unit, a sound-attenuating screening suite serving underserved communities across Minnesota. Over 1,000 screenings completed so far.

The rubber hand illusion with a live cockroach

Gustavus Adolphus College · Mentor: Lauren Hecht · 2022–2023

My first research project: two semesters designing an experiment on entomophobic responses during the rubber hand illusion, in collaboration with the Gustavus Biology Department. Presented as a talk at Spring Research Day.
