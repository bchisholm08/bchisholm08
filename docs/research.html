Research

Research Focus

I am interested in how listeners extract meaningful signals from competing sound, and how that ability changes with hearing loss, age, or auditory prostheses. I have approached this question at several levels: spatial and comodulation masking release in an animal model, speech-in-speech perception and listening effort in human listeners with and without mild cognitive impairment, and pitch coding in cochlear-implant and auditory-nerve-implant recipients.

Methodologically, I pair behavioral psychophysics with physiological measures — EEG and pupillometry — and analyze the resulting data with mixed-effects and generalized additive models in R and MATLAB.

Current Projects

Psychophysics for an auditory nerve implant

In the Sensory Optimization & Neural Implant Coding Lab (PI: Dr. Hubert Lim), I validate a six-module psychophysical test suite for an auditory nerve implant, verifying stimulus output and response hardware before protocols are released to the collaborating clinical team in Hannover, Germany. I also lead recruitment and data collection for a pitch-scaling study in normal-hearing and cochlear-implant listeners that establishes comparison data for the same task in implant recipients.

Listening effort and fatigue in speech-in-noise

In the Auditory Perception & Cognition Lab (PI: Dr. Andrew Oxenham), I studied speech-in-speech perception using EEG and pupillometry, including work with listeners experiencing mild cognitive impairment. My first-author manuscript asks whether pupillometric fatigue interacts with experimental manipulation in a speech-in-noise listening effort task.

Masking release in an animal model

In the Animal Communication Lab (PI: Dr. Mark Bee), I contributed to research on spatial release from masking and comodulation masking release in Cope's gray treefrog — the same perceptual phenomena studied in human speech-in-noise research, in a comparative model of signal detection in noisy, multi-talker environments.

Community hearing and vision screening

Through the Center for Applied & Translational Sensory Science, I administer hearing and vision screenings aboard the See, Hear, MN mobile sensory unit, a sound-attenuating screening suite serving underserved communities across Minnesota. Over 1,000 screenings completed to date.
